Detect Conflicts
Before They Happen

Check whether instructions can coexist before asking an AI to follow them. A hybrid rule-based and LLM-driven framework powered by NVIDIA NIM for detecting and resolving conflicting user instructions.

Sample Conflict: "Write exactly 100 words, but use fewer than 80 words."
Compatible
Requirements appear jointly satisfiable. All instructions can coexist without conflict.
Partially Conflicting
Soft goals compete; a compromise may work but requires careful priority decisions.
Fully Impossible
Hard requirements cannot all hold in the same scope. Logical contradiction detected.
Underspecified
Essential input is missing. Clarification needed before the prompt can be executed.

Ablation Comparison

Three detector ablations: rules-only, LLM-only, hybrid. Evaluated across numerical, logical, temporal, formatting, and semantic cases separately. Successful resolution and full instruction satisfaction need independent ratings. Empty metrics mean unmeasured, not zero.

100 Critical Contradiction Benchmark & Model Fine-Tuning Corpus

A comprehensive 100-prompt benchmark dataset testing LLM sensitivity and rule verification across 5 major contradiction paradigms: Structural & Length, Semantic Paradoxes, Persona Clashes, Negated Execution, and Dependency Traps.

1. Hard Structural & Length (20)
Mathematical interval clashes (10-page thesis in 3 words; 10,000 words yet under 50 words; detailed manual yet entirely blank).
2. Semantic Definition Paradoxes (20)
Axiomatic and physical impossibilities (underwater yet bone-dry desert; silent sound yet deafening; freezing water by boiling).
3. Identity & Persona Clashes (20)
Irreconcilable registers (mournful funeral eulogy in celebratory lottery tone; silent mime narrating podcast; surgical report in toddler language).
4. Input-Output Negation & Rules (20)
Demanding execution while forbidding necessary tools (translate to French without French words; calculate sum without math).
5. Multi-Step & Dependency Traps (20)
Circular causal loops and destructive chains (write then delete all words then analyze; blind character describing visual sights).

Methodology

The implementation uses deterministic interval and polarity checks plus structured NVIDIA NIM LLM analysis. Repairs require explicit user approval. No custom training, RAG, or external benchmark data is required.

Known Limitations
  • Finite English patterns — limited grammar coverage
  • Incomplete scope/conditional reasoning
  • Synthetic template bias in evaluation
  • Provisional semantic labels
  • Model-dependent semantic judgments
  • Do not interpret a clean offline result as universal proof
